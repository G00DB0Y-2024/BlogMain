### Reviewer: 3

Comments to the Author
To the best of my knowledge, this paper is addressing a quite original solution: casting MARL cooperation for Ising-Heuristic Routing with a learnable coupling field. The system model is relatively well defined, in terms of constellation, link, queueing, and outage model, also considering the space limitations for a letter. The benchmark suite and statistical reporting are sound and reasonable, providing some nice validation.

Overall the paper is also quite well written and clear.

Despite the above positive aspects, there are major concerns on the technical soundness of this work:
\- First, some system-level parameters really tend to be implausible / currently unfeasible. The authors consider antenna gains in the order of 120 dBi, working at 23.28 GHz. To achieve this at such a "low" frequency, the antenna aperture would be quite massive compared to current LEO satellites (orders of magnitude larger); realistic ISL terminals operate can achieve up to 60-70 dBi. Such an antenna gain might be achievable, maybe at Terahertz frequencies. Similarly, a noise spectral density of -180 dBm/Hz is far below thermal noise at any reasonable system temperature for ISL: the Boltzmann constant provides -228.6 dBW/K/Hz, which means -198.6 dBm/K/Hz, and to achieve the -180 considered by the authors we need a noise equivalent temperature of 72.4 K, i.e., -200.7 °C. I understand that we are in space, but to achieve this temperature one would need cryogenic refrigeration with liquid nitrogen, not used to the best of my knowledge. Typical values, for instance Starlink, are in the order of 180K, leading to a noise density of -176 approximately. This is a major issue for this paper, as all link budget evaluations, and thus the results, are to be re-computed and re-validated.
\- The considered packet size of 1.5 MB is a bit unconventional. If we look at typical IP MTUs we have 1500 B, which is 3 orders of magnitude below. If the authors refer to "packet” as to a logical traffic unit or application-layer chunk, the paper should clarify this. If this is not the case, the transmission delay is largely overstated roughly by a factor x1000 and it undermines the latency numbers, as the link budget above.
\- The action space is too restrictive: limiting the action set to the four planar neighbours (north, south, east, west) is reasonable for a grid-like Walker-Delta constellation. However, it ignores a major real world complication: cross-seam routing and the absence of West ISLs across the counter-rotating seam, plus polar-region disconnections. As for the latter, the authors explicitly model polar regions in the channel layer but this does not reconcile with the fixed 4 neighbour MDP. A short discussion of how the action space adapts when some of the four directions are unavailable is really needed.
\- The comparison with the State-of-the-Art is a bit incomplete, as several recent and directly relevant satellite-routing works are cited but not compared against (in particular references [2], [3], and [8]). Comparing against at least one of these graph-aware satellite-specific schemes is necessary to sustain the proposed solution.
\- The convergence and training cost are not discussed. Fig. 3(a) shows a convergence in roughly 500 episodes on an RTX 5080 with a 13th gen i9, but how long is an episode in wall-clock and in simulated time? How does training time scale with MxN? Moreover, the on-board inference cost (GRU + Dueling DQN per slot) is not discussed / compared to what is feasible on a real micro-satellite payloads today. For a routing scheme aimed at distributed deployment, this is quite critical.

Based on the above, I do not think the paper can be published as, despite the good ideas, several deep corrections, and in some cases re-thinking, are needed.
