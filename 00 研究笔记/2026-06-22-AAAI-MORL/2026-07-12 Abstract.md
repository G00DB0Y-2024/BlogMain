Pareto Q-learning (PQL) introduced by Van Moffaert and Nowé fundamentally advances Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning (MORL) by explicitly maintaining non-dominated Q-vector sets to extract Pareto-optimal policies without relying on predefined scalarization weights, thereby successfully overcoming the limitations of non-convex Pareto fronts. However, its tabular nature restricts its scalability to continuous/large-scale state spaces. This paper proposes Deep Pareto Q-learning (DeepPQL) as a neural extension of PQL. Theoretically, we establish a rigorous foundation for set approximation by proving the contraction and convergence of the Pareto Bellman operator via mapping Pareto fronts to downward closure spaces under the Hausdorff metric. Methodologically, DeepPQL bootstraps multi-objective candidate Q-sets from target networks and utilizes non-dominated filtering to yield target distributions, elegantly transforming the challenges of discrete set distance metrics and element correspondence into a differentiable, continuous generative distribution matching process. Furthermore, we systematically investigate adversarial, variational, and diffusional parameterizations, revealing the distinct performance differences among these models in terms of mode collapse, Pareto front coverage, and training stability. Experimental results confirm that DeepPQL successfully inherits the weight-free, non-convex exploration capabilities of traditional PQL while significantly extending its applicability and scalability in deep multi-objective reinforcement learning scenarios.
