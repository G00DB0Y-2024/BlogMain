## A. Proofs for the Exact Pareto Bellman Operator

In this section, we provide the detailed proofs for the lemmas and theorems presented in the theoretical analysis, establishing the topological convergence of the exact Pareto Q-Learning algorithm.

### Proof of Lemma 1 (Isomorphism and Operator Equivalence)

*Proof.* We first establish the perfect bijection between the Pareto frontier space $\mathcal{F}$ and the comprehensive hull space $\mathcal{C}$. Define the mapping $f: \mathcal{F} \rightarrow \mathcal{C}$ as $f(Q) = \mathcal{D}(Q)$, and the inverse mapping $g: \mathcal{C} \rightarrow \mathcal{F}$ as $g(C) = ND(C)$.

For any strict Pareto frontier $Q \in \mathcal{F}$, taking its downward closure and then extracting the non-dominated maximal boundary strictly recovers the original frontier, i.e., $g(f(Q)) = ND(\mathcal{D}(Q)) = Q$. Conversely, for any comprehensive hull $C \in \mathcal{C}$, the downward closure of its non-dominated boundary recovers the entire hull, i.e., $f(g(C)) = \mathcal{D}(ND(C)) = C$. Thus, $f$ and $g$ form a perfect bijection.

Since the metric on $\mathcal{F}$ is explicitly induced by the Hausdorff distance on $\mathcal{C}$ via $d_{\mathcal{F}}(Q_1, Q_2) \triangleq d_H(\mathcal{D}(Q_1), \mathcal{D}(Q_2)) = d_H(f(Q_1), f(Q_2))$, the mapping $f$ strictly preserves distances, making the metric spaces $(\mathcal{F}, d_{\mathcal{F}})$ and $(\mathcal{C}, d_H)$ isometrically isomorphic.

Regarding operator equivalence, let $Q \in \mathcal{F}$ and apply the Pareto Bellman operator $\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}$:

$$\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q) = \mathbf{r} \oplus \gamma ND \left( \bigcup Q' \right)$$

Since $\mathcal{D}(ND(X)) \equiv \mathcal{D}(X)$ for any set $X$, and the Minkowski addition of constants is translation-invariant under closure, applying the downward closure operator to both sides yields:

$$\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q)) = \mathcal{D}(\mathbf{r}) \oplus \gamma \mathcal{D} \left( \bigcup \mathcal{D}(Q') \right) = \mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{D}(Q))$$

Applying the $ND$ operator to both sides recovers the equivalence: $\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q) = ND(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{D}(Q)))$. Thus, $\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}} \equiv ND \circ \mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}} \circ \mathcal{D}$, confirming topological equivalence. $\blacksquare$

### Proof of Theorem 1 (Convergence and Unique Fixed Point)

*Proof.* To prove convergence, we must demonstrate that the continuous operator $\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}$ is a strict $\gamma$-contraction in the complete metric space $(\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{S} \times \mathcal{A}}, d_H)$. For any two comprehensive hulls $C_1, C_2 \in \mathcal{C}$, the Hausdorff distance exhibits the following non-expansive properties:

1. **Translation Invariance:** $d_H(A \oplus C, B \oplus C) \le d_H(A, B)$.
2. **Scaling:** $d_H(\gamma A, \gamma B) = \gamma d_H(A, B)$.
3. **Union Non-Expansiveness:** $d_H(\bigcup A_i, \bigcup B_i) \le \max_i d_H(A_i, B_i)$.

Let the maximum supremum distance across the state-action space be denoted as $D_\infty(C_1, C_2) = \max_{s,a} d_H(C_1(s,a), C_2(s,a))$. Applying the operator $\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}$ to $C_1$ and $C_2$ for an arbitrary state-action pair $(s,a)$:

$$\begin{aligned} d_H \Big( \mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(C_1)(s,a), \mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(C_2)(s,a) \Big)  &= d_H \left( \mathcal{D}(\mathbf{r}) \oplus \gamma \mathbb{E}_{s'} \left[ \bigcup_{a'} C_1 \right], \mathcal{D}(\mathbf{r}) \oplus \gamma \mathbb{E}_{s'} \left[ \bigcup_{a'} C_2 \right] \right) \\ &\le \gamma \cdot d_H \left( \mathbb{E}_{s'} \left[ \bigcup_{a'} C_1 \right], \mathbb{E}_{s'} \left[ \bigcup_{a'} C_2 \right] \right) \quad \text{(Properties 1 \& 2)} \\ &\le \gamma \sum_{s'} P(s'\vert{}s,a) \cdot d_H \left( \bigcup_{a'} C_1(s',a'), \bigcup_{a'} C_2(s',a') \right) \\ &\le \gamma \max_{s'} \max_{a'} d_H(C_1(s',a'), C_2(s',a')) \quad \text{(Property 3)} \\ &\le \gamma D_\infty(C_1, C_2) \end{aligned}$$

Since this holds for all $(s,a)$, we have $D_\infty(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}C_1, \mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}C_2) \le \gamma D_\infty(C_1, C_2)$. Given $\gamma \in (0, 1)$, $\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}$ is a strict contraction mapping. According to the Banach Fixed-Point Theorem, there exists a unique fixed comprehensive hull $C^* \in \mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{S} \times \mathcal{A}}$. By the isometric isomorphism established in Lemma 1, $Q^* = ND(C^*)$ is the uniquely guaranteed fixed point of the Pareto operator $\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}$ in $\mathcal{F}^{\mathcal{S} \times \mathcal{A}}$. $\blacksquare$

### Proof of Theorem 2 (Global Pareto Optimality)

*Proof.* Let $\mathcal{Q}(s,a)$ denote the true global feasible objective space, which encompasses all expected returns generated by any sequence of policies. By the Markovian property, the feasible space can be recursively decomposed into immediate rewards and future feasible returns:

$$\mathcal{Q}(s,a) = \mathbf{r}(s,a) \oplus \gamma \mathbb{E}_{s' \sim P} \left[ \bigcup_{a' \in \mathcal{A}} \mathcal{Q}(s', a') \right]$$

To obtain the globally optimal Pareto frontier $Q_{true}^*$, we apply the non-dominated operator $ND(\cdot)$ to both sides:

$$Q_{true}^*(s,a) = ND(\mathcal{Q}(s,a)) = ND \left( \mathbf{r}(s,a) \oplus \gamma \mathbb{E}_{s'} \left[ \bigcup_{a'} \mathcal{Q}(s', a') \right] \right)$$

The $ND(\cdot)$ operator satisfies two critical algebraic properties: translation invariance $ND(\mathbf{r} \oplus X) = \mathbf{r} \oplus ND(X)$, and sub-optimality filtering $ND(\bigcup X) = ND(\bigcup ND(X))$. Distributing the $ND$ operator through the Minkowski addition and the union operation yields:

$$Q_{true}^*(s,a) = \mathbf{r}(s,a) \oplus \gamma ND \left( \mathbb{E}_{s'} \left[ \bigcup_{a'} ND(\mathcal{Q}(s', a')) \right] \right)$$

Substituting $Q_{true}^*(s',a') = ND(\mathcal{Q}(s',a'))$ back into the right-hand side, we obtain:

$$Q_{true}^*(s,a) = \mathbf{r}(s,a) \oplus \gamma ND \left( \mathbb{E}_{s'} \left[ \bigcup_{a'} Q_{true}^*(s', a') \right] \right) \equiv \mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q_{true}^*)$$

This equation demonstrates that the true global Pareto frontier $Q_{true}^*$ strictly satisfies the Pareto Bellman equation. Given that Theorem 1 guarantees the absolute uniqueness of the fixed point for $\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}$, the convergence limit $Q^*$ of our algorithm is exactly $Q_{true}^*$. $\blacksquare$

## B. Proofs for Probabilistic Set Parameterization in DeepPQL

In this section, we provide detailed proofs for the error propagation bounds and probabilistic guarantees of DeepPQL, effectively bridging the continuous Wasserstein probability measure with the macroscopic Hausdorff geometry.

### Proof of Lemma 2 (Support Mapping and Closure Error Bound)

*Proof.* Under Assumption 2, the generative model satisfies $W_1(p_\theta, p^*) \le \epsilon$. The 1-Wasserstein distance is defined as the infimum of the expected cost over all joint distributions $\pi(x, y)$ with marginals $p_\theta$ and $p^*$: $W_1(p_\theta, p^*) = \inf_{\pi} \mathbb{E}_{(x,y) \sim \pi} [\vert{}\vert{}\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y}\vert{}\vert{}_2]$.

For distributions with bounded continuous densities on their supports, the spatial deviation between their supports is strictly bounded by their Wasserstein metric up to a regularity constant $c > 0$, yielding:

$$d_H(\text{supp}(p_\theta), \text{supp}(p^*)) \le c \cdot W_1(p_\theta, p^*) \le c\epsilon$$

Recall that $Q_{k+1} = ND(\text{supp}(p_\theta))$ and $\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q_k) = ND(\text{supp}(p^*))$. The topological distance in the Pareto frontier space $\mathcal{F}$ is defined as $d_{\mathcal{F}}(A, B) = d_H(\mathcal{D}(A), \mathcal{D}(B))$.

Substituting the frontiers, we have:

$$d_\mathcal{F}(Q_{k+1}, \mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q_k)) = d_H(\mathcal{D}(ND(\text{supp}(p_\theta))), \mathcal{D}(ND(\text{supp}(p^*))))$$

By the identity established in Lemma 1, $\mathcal{D}(ND(X)) \equiv \mathcal{D}(X)$. Furthermore, applying the downward closure operator to any two sets strictly avoids spatial expansion, satisfying $d_H(\mathcal{D}(A), \mathcal{D}(B)) \le d_H(A, B)$. Therefore:

$$d_H(\mathcal{D}(\text{supp}(p_\theta)), \mathcal{D}(\text{supp}(p^*))) \le d_H(\text{supp}(p_\theta), \text{supp}(p^*)) \le c\epsilon$$

Thus, $d_\mathcal{F}(Q_{k+1}, \mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q_k)) \le c\epsilon$, rigorously translating the probabilistic error into a bounded geometric hull deviation. $\blacksquare$

### Proof of Theorem 3 (Asymptotic Convergence Bound of DeepPQL)

*Proof.* We analyze the geometric error propagation in the complete metric space $(\mathcal{C}, d_H)$ using approximate dynamic programming (ADP). Let $C_{k+1} = \mathcal{D}(Q_{k+1})$ be the generated comprehensive hull at step $k+1$, and $C^*$ be the true optimal comprehensive hull. By applying the triangle inequality in $(\mathcal{C}, d_H)$:

$$d_H(C_{k+1}, C^*) \le d_H(C_{k+1}, \mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(C_k)) + d_H(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(C_k), \mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(C^*))$$

For the first term on the right-hand side, based on the operator equivalence $\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(C_k) = \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q_k))$, we apply Lemma 2 to obtain:

$$d_H(C_{k+1}, \mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(C_k)) = d_H(\mathcal{D}(Q_{k+1}), \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q_k))) \le c\epsilon$$

For the second term, Theorem 1 guarantees that the exact Bellman operator $\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}$ is a strict $\gamma$-contraction, ensuring:

$$d_H(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(C_k), \mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(C^*)) \le \gamma d_H(C_k, C^*)$$

Substituting these two bounds back into the triangle inequality yields a recursive relationship:

$$d_H(C_{k+1}, C^*) \le c\epsilon + \gamma d_H(C_k, C^*)$$

Unrolling this inequality recursively to infinity, the initial geometric distance $d_H(C_0, C^*)$ decays to zero, and the accumulated generative error resolves to a geometric series:

$$\limsup_{k \to \infty} d_H(C_k, C^*) \le \sum_{i=0}^{\infty} \gamma^i (c\epsilon) = \frac{c\epsilon}{1-\gamma}$$

Given the definition $d_{\mathcal{F}}(Q_k, Q^*) \triangleq d_H(C_k, C^*)$, the asymptotic bound in the frontier space $\mathcal{F}$ is established. $\blacksquare$

### Proof of Theorem 4 (Probabilistic Non-Domination)

*Proof.* By Theorem 3, the generated sequence of frontiers asymptotically converges to a bounded neighborhood of the true optimal frontier $Q^*$. Let $\Delta_{max} = \frac{c\epsilon}{1-\gamma}$ represent this maximum supremum Hausdorff distance constraint.

Consequently, for any distribution induced by the converged model $p_\theta$, the underlying continuous density dictates that the expected projection distance of a generated sample $\mathbf{q} \sim p_\theta$ to the optimal frontier $Q^*$ is upper-bounded by $\Delta_{max}$:

$$\mathbb{E}_{p_\theta} \left[ \inf_{\mathbf{y} \in Q^*} \vert{}\vert{}\mathbf{q} - \mathbf{y}\vert{}\vert{}_2 \right] \le \Delta_{max}$$

Because the Euclidean distance $X = \inf_{\mathbf{y} \in Q^*} \vert{}\vert{}\mathbf{q} - \mathbf{y}\vert{}\vert{}_2$ is a strictly non-negative random variable, we can directly apply Markov's Inequality, which states that for any $X \ge 0$ and strictly positive threshold $\delta > 0$, $P(X \ge \delta) \le \frac{\mathbb{E}[X]}{\delta}$.

Substituting the bounded expected distance yields:

$$P\left(\inf_{\mathbf{y} \in Q^*} \vert{}\vert{}\mathbf{q} - \mathbf{y}\vert{}\vert{}_2 \ge \delta\right) \le \frac{\Delta_{max}}{\delta} = \frac{c\epsilon}{\delta(1-\gamma)}$$

This theoretically verifies that given sufficient training to minimize $\epsilon$, the generative likelihood of sub-optimal deviation sharply decays, ensuring that applying the deterministic $ND(\cdot)$ operator to the empirical samples will efficiently extract a robust Pareto optimal policy. $\blacksquare$
